Purpose

A first look at how land use changes from downtown Richmond to Pocahontas State Park. The study area is cut into non-overlapping 1 km windows along the transect, each spanning the full 4 km buffer width, and each window is summarized from the Annual NLCD 2025 land cover and fractional impervious surface layers (30 m) and from NDVI computed from the 4-band (RGB + near-infrared) 12-inch imagery.

NLCD is kept on its native grid (EPSG:5070). Windows are projected to it and summarized with exactextractr, which weights each 30 m cell by the fraction of it inside the window.

Evergreen cover follows the urban-to-forest gradient only loosely. It is lowest in downtown Richmond (about 7–9% at km 0–2), sits between about 12% and 22% through most of the transect, and peaks at 43% at km 21–22, where pine stands are common. In the developed windows part of the signal is green winter lawn and street trees rather than evergreen canopy; the adjusted index is calibrated on forest, so it is least reliable on bright urban surfaces.

Within forest the evergreen share does not follow the urban gradient. The small forest patches near km 9–11 are pine-rich (41–46% evergreen), the forests at km 12–20 are mostly deciduous (18–28%), km 21–22 is pine-rich again (54%), and km 23–24 is hardwood-dominated (about 19%). The amount of forest (NLCD) and its composition (NDVI) change at different places along the transect. That matters for holly: an isolated evergreen crown is easiest to see, and most likely to be holly, in deciduous forest. Windows 1–4 have no NLCD forest, so they have no forest-only value.

Notes

  • Data: Annual NLCD Collection 1, 2025 land cover (LndCov) and fractional impervious surface (FctImp), read for the AOI bounding box from the USGS web coverage service (https://dmsdata.cr.usgs.gov/geoserver). No resampling or reprojection of the rasters.
  • Developed combines NLCD classes 22–24 (low, medium, high intensity). Developed open space (21) is mostly lawns, parks, and road verges and is kept separate. Forest is classes 41–43; wetland is 90 and 95.
  • NDVI: computed at full 1 ft resolution from the 4-band VBMP tiles and summarized into 10 ft cells (mean NDVI and share of pixels above the evergreen threshold) before the window summaries. NLCD is resampled to the 10 ft grid (nearest neighbour) only to mask water and select forest. The NIR band is not radiometrically calibrated, so NDVI values are relative to this imagery, and leaf-off timing means NDVI says little about deciduous canopy.
  • Windows are not replicated samples. Adjacent windows are spatially autocorrelated, so the curves describe the gradient but should not be treated as 25 independent observations.
  • The 4 km width averages across very different ground on either side of the line (e.g. the James River crossing near 5–7 km). A narrower center band would test how much that matters.
